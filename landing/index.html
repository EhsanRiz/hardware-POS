Scope

A counter screen does four things.

Scan, bill, take payment, print. Stock movement shows as a consequence on each line, never as a task. Quotes, statements, age analysis, branch transfers and stock takes live one tab away — a screen that tries to hold every operational reality at once is precisely why the incumbent hardware software is disliked.

Cut to length

Rebar cut to 2.4 m records how many stock lengths it consumed and what offcut is left — on the line, at the moment of sale, where a dispute later can still be answered.

Weighed off the scale

Loose bin stock reads from the scale, subtracts the tare, and records which scale it came from. If the mass is keyed by hand, the line says so — these are the lines that get argued about.

Stock, shown not managed

Each line carries its own movement: stock 148 → 128. The cashier sees the consequence without being handed a second job.

Why this matters here

Built for the connection you actually have.

A till that needs the internet is a till that closes when the internet does. Load shedding means a counter may run for hours offline and then sync hundreds of transactions at once. That is a design requirement, not an edge case.

Sell through the outage

The catalogue, both price bands, account limits and the VAT rate all live on the till. Nobody stands at the counter waiting for a spinner.

Reconcile without duplicates

Every sale carries a key made on the device. If a request committed but its reply was lost, the retry resolves to the same sale rather than charging the customer a second time.

Honest paperwork

A slip printed offline says its invoice number is pending rather than inventing one. The sequential number is issued the moment the sale reaches the server.

Questions

The things shops ask first.

What happens if the internet goes down mid-sale?

Nothing stops. The catalogue is on the till, so the sale completes and the invoice prints. It is stored on the device and synced when the line returns — with a key that prevents the same sale being recorded twice.

Do we need special hardware?

No. A desktop till or a landscape tablet, and a thermal slip printer. A USB or Bluetooth barcode scanner behaves as a keyboard, so scanning needs no extra integration.

Is it right for SARS?

Invoices carry sequential numbers and your VAT registration, and the VAT amount is stored as it was charged, so a reprint years later shows the original figures. If you trade in Maloti as well as Rand near the border, tell us — that affects the drawer and the cash-up.

Can we keep our own product codes?

Yes. Products carry your SKU and, where one exists, a barcode. Import matches on your SKU, so a repeat import updates rather than duplicates.

Who can see cost prices?

Only staff you grant it to. Seeing costs is a separate permission from managing products, so a supervisor can fix a price or a barcode without being shown the shop's margins.

What does it cost?

It depends on the number of tills and how much setup your price list needs. Get in touch and we will quote against your actual range rather than a generic tier.
